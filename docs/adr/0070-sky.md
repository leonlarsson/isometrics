# 0070. A sky around the world in perspective, and a backdrop of the world's own behind the diorama

## Context and problem statement

Free camera control and flying (ADR-0062) let people look up in perspective, but above the world there was only the page's background, tinted by the time of day (ADR-0052). The isometric diorama, on the other hand, is meant to float over a plain colour, and people want to choose that colour, a gradient, or a green screen to film the world and key it out.

## Decision drivers

- Keep the diorama's look as it is unless the world chooses otherwise; existing worlds look the same there (ADR-0056).
- A sky that follows the time of day, with the sun where the light comes from.
- Every part customizable, with Default / Reset like every other setting (ADR-0027).
- Cheap to draw: one dome, a few dozen low-poly clouds.

## Considered options

1. A sky in perspective only; a separate backdrop setting for the diorama.
2. A sky in both views.
3. A world setting choosing between a plain background and a sky, for both views.

## Decision outcome

Chosen: **a sky in perspective, a backdrop for the diorama** (1).

- **The sky** (`scene.sky`, scene/sky.ts, render/SkyDome and render/Clouds): a dome around the camera, sized just inside the far plane (which follows the camera's distance) and drawn behind everything, with a gradient from the horizon to overhead for the time of day (its own keyframes; a soft blue in classic daylight), the sun's disc and glow where the sunlight comes from (Sun & sky's direction and height count), the moon opposite it at night, stars fading in with the dark, and flat-shaded low-poly clouds on a wide ring above the world, drifting while motion plays, tinted by the light and a moonlit grey-blue at night. Settings: `showInPerspective` (off shows the backdrop there too), `colorOverhead` and `colorAtHorizon` (following the time of day until set), `showSun`, `showMoon`, `starDensity`, `cloudCover`, `cloudColor` and `cloudSpeed`.
- **The backdrop** (`scene.backdrop`, ui/AppShell's stage): what's behind the world in the diorama: the sky's colour (as before, the default), a colour, a gradient between two colours at any angle (`gradientStart`, `gradientEnd`, `gradientAngle`; top to bottom by default), or a green screen (#00ff00). The world's title turns light on a dark backdrop, as on a night sky. A picture may come later.
- Both are in Sun & sky, as the Sky and Backdrop panels.

### Consequences

- Good: perspective, riding along and flying get a horizon, a sun and a night sky; the diorama can match a brand colour or be filmed against a green screen.
- Bad: worlds viewed in perspective look different than before (a sky instead of the page's colour); switching the sky off brings the old look back.
- Later: clouds and haze could follow data (cloud cover from a weather value), with *Weather* (TODO).
