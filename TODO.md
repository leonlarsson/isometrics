## Developer experience

- [ ] **Hot reload resets and pauses the world.** After a code change in development, the world goes back to the starter world (or as it was) and motion pauses; only a reload helps. Cause: `useEditor` is created when `editor/store.ts` loads, and the store imports the parts, templates and scene code, so changing any of them makes Vite re-run `store.ts` and create a new store with the starter world and default state; the saved world is only loaded when the app first mounts. The old store also keeps the autosave subscription, so edits after a hot reload aren't saved until a reload (development only, but a quiet way to lose work). Fix idea: keep the store's state across hot updates (`import.meta.hot.data`: hand the scene, history, mode, selection and playing state to the new store, and move autosave over), or make the store module accept hot updates without re-creating the store.

## Ideas backlog (2026-10-21)

Ideas from the owner, not designed yet. Each needs a design pass (and usually an ADR) before building. Per ADR-0019, note how each could be driven by data.

- [ ] **Turning autosave off:** the save status next to the File menu is done (ADR-0057). Maybe a setting to turn autosave off (per browser or per world), e.g. to try changes and throw them away: it then needs an "Unsaved changes" state in that status, an explicit "Save in this browser" (Ctrl+S's meaning would need a decision), and a warning before closing the tab or opening another world with unsaved changes, so nothing is lost by accident (ADR-0020). Needs an ADR. *Data:* once worlds load live data, the status could also say when the data was last updated.
- [ ] **People:** pedestrians as a part, walking along paths, pavements or hidden routes; maybe standing crowds. *Data:* crowd size or walking speed from a value (visitors, queue length).
- [ ] **Helicopters and planes:** aircraft as parts that fly along hidden routes in the air (take-off, circuits, landing on a helipad or runway), using the route heights from ADR-0018. They should bank into turns like real aircraft (roll towards the inside of the curve, more for tighter or faster turns), and pitch when climbing or descending (a helicopter also noses down to fly forward). Banking needs roll, which entities don't have yet (see *Rotation in all three axes*), though it could be applied at render time only. To make this easier, let a whole route (road, path or hidden route) be dragged at once instead of point by point, so a flight path can be moved or lifted in one go. Also an **airport** building (terminal, control tower, runway or apron), plus a helipad, for them to take off from and land at. *Data:* flights per hour or flying speed from a value; passengers per day on the airport.
- [ ] **Building clusters:** one object that grows with a number: more, taller or bigger buildings as the value rises (abstract; needs exploring). *Data:* naturally driven by a value, e.g. revenue or headcount per city block.
- [ ] **Many objects:** grounds now go up to 500 per side (ADR-0049), so a world can hold far more objects than the parts were built for: each part is its own set of meshes, with no instancing. Render repeated parts (trees, houses, lamps) with instancing once a big world gets slow. *Data:* bigger worlds hold more label cards; the label layout (ADR-0048) shrinks the crowded ones to chips.
- [ ] **Multiple areas / cities (SimCity-like):** several districts or grounds in one world, maybe linked by roads, each with its own name and data. Affects the scene model (ground is a single slab today), camera framing and navigation between areas.
- [ ] **Weather:** fog, rain, snow, maybe clouds. Scene-level setting, possibly per area. *Data:* weather from a value or a live feed.
- [ ] **Lighting system, next:** lights are a shared system now (ADR-0058): parts declare their lights, each object sets when, colour, strength and reach per light, data can switch or dim them, and a Light part places one anywhere. They're still painted on (glowing surfaces and pools on the ground), not real lights. Still to do: lights that light up their surroundings (walls, cars, trees), especially at dusk and night; more shapes (an aimed spotlight or floodlight beam, a glowing sign, a blinking beacon); and data setting a light's colour (e.g. red past a threshold). Mind performance: many real lights are expensive in three.js, so likely a few real ones plus the cheap painted ones.
- [ ] **More camera freedom:** orbit around the cursor, a perspective view, saved viewpoints, and following or riding along with a vehicle are done (ADR-0037–0040). Still to do: a **first-person mode** to free-walk with WASD + mouse look.
- [ ] **Better camera controls:** settings for the camera, likely a Camera tab in the properties (ADR-0054). Ideas: **field of view** for the perspective camera (fixed at 40° today, `PERSPECTIVE_FOV`), saved with each viewpoint; zoom limits and how far it can tilt; orbit, pan and zoom speed, with inverted zoom/scroll; how smooth (damped) movement feels; how long the glide between viewpoints takes; keyboard panning (arrow keys or WASD) and keyboard zoom; keeping the camera over the ground; an auto-rotating turntable in View mode; a tilt-shift (depth of field) blur that sells the miniature look; and a composition grid overlay for lining up viewpoints. Per-world settings belong in the scene, personal ones (speeds, inverted zoom) per browser. Builds on ADR-0028, ADR-0037–0040. *Data:* a tour that steps through viewpoints on its own, or one that flies to whatever crossed a threshold.
- [ ] **Take control of a vehicle or person:** riding along (view only) is done (ADR-0040); next, actually drive or walk it yourself (keyboard/gamepad), leaving its road while you steer and rejoining afterwards. Needs people as a part for walking.
- [ ] **More traffic behaviour:** following and overtaking are done (ADR-0042). Still to do: multi-lane roads (two lanes per direction, passing without the oncoming lane), vehicles meeting head-on on roads without lanes (a ferry route driven both ways), and maybe a per-road "no overtaking" setting. *Data:* traffic density or speeds from values.
- [ ] **Connected road networks (junctions):** roads that join at intersections, with vehicles routing across roads instead of staying on one. Builds on ADR-0017/0018; the current "join" merges two roads into one but doesn't create junctions.
- [ ] **Window lights: a split slider.** Let people set how a building's windows are split between its window lights (the office tower's Windows and Screens, and any added later): one bar divided into a segment per light, dividers to drag, stored as a `weight` per light in the object's light settings (null = the part's, ADR-0027). Weights are relative, so the bar always fills 100% and can't overflow; each light's Lit panes stays a share of its own windows. Changing the split reshuffles which panes are whose, so say so in the hint. Weights could later follow data too (ADR-0058).
- [ ] **Time of day panel: "Time follows" first.** In Sun & sky → Time of day, the *Time follows* select (the time set here / a day cycle / the viewer's clock / a data value) decides what the rest of the panel means, so it belongs at the top, with the time slider and presets under it.
- [ ] **Data tab: search, and where each value is used.** A search box over the values (name, key, value), and for each value the places that use it: label cards, detail tiles, parts' data fields (the text part), lights' data bindings (ADR-0058) and the time of day's data source, each a link that selects the object or opens the setting. Also warns before deleting a value that's in use. *Note:* the owner plans to redesign the whole data flow at some point, so keep this light.
- [ ] **Show the route of a selected vehicle:** selecting something that drives along a road should highlight that road or route, so it's clear where it goes (hidden routes especially, which are otherwise invisible). Could reuse the arrows shown while dragging a vehicle onto a road (ADR-0031), drawn along its route in its direction of travel. *Data:* the arrows could run faster or slower with a value (traffic speed).
- [ ] **Label cards and the side panels:** cards keep fully inside the 3D canvas (ADR-0048), and the canvas ends where a side panel starts, so cards near that edge step inwards when a panel opens or widens: they seem to avoid the panels. Options: let cards overlap the panels (the label layer drawn above them, outside the canvas), or let them go off the edge like the world does.
- [ ] **Areas, rethought: raising and per-corner heights.** Every area type can already be raised: "Raised by" (in the area's Shape tab, ADR-0054) works for water, grass, sand and plazas too, and the mesh extrudes any type with cliff sides. "Raised land" is only special in starting at 1.5 when drawn (`startHeight` in `scene/areas.ts`). That's easy to miss and muddles two ideas, the surface (what it's made of) and the shape (flat or raised). Decide whether "raised" should be a shape setting for every surface (and "Raised land" become a land surface that starts raised), make raising visible where people look (the Area tab, a lift handle in the scene like objects' green arrow), and check what raised water should look like. **Per-corner lift:** a height per corner, like road points have (ADR-0018): Shift+drag or the green arrow on a selected corner, and a Height field in its Corner panel, for slopes, ramps and hills instead of flat-topped blocks. Needs the top triangulated with each corner's height and the cliff sides following it, objects and roads standing on the sloped top (pairs with "terrain that roads and entities snap to" below), and a schema change (`points` as `[x, z]` or `[x, z, height]`, like roads). Needs an ADR. *Data:* a corner's or the whole area's height from a value (a rising water level, a stockpile's size).
- [ ] **Area polish (ADR-0021):** an animated water surface and shoreline, holes (an island in a lake), smoother outlines, and terrain that roads and entities snap to on raised land.
- [ ] **Ground textures:** more than a flat colour for the ground and areas: grass, sand, gravel, concrete, snow…, possibly with subtle patterns (a texture or a procedural shader that fits the low-poly look). Start with a surface type for the whole ground and for zones. Paintable ground (brushing textures freely) is probably too much for now; zones with surface types cover most of it. *Data:* surface could reflect state (e.g. snow by season, a field's colour by harvest).
- [ ] **Rotation in all three axes:** objects only turn around the vertical axis (pitch exists, −60–60°, but is only set automatically on ramps). Needs a design pass and an ADR: yaw/pitch/roll in degrees in the schema (readable for people and AIs), keeping tilted objects on the ground (lift by the tilted bounding box), footprints and selection frames that assume an upright box, gizmo rings for X and Z, keyboard R with X/Y/Z axis locks and snapped turns, how it combines with a vehicle's road tilt, and where label cards anchor. A cheap first step: pitch and roll sliders in the inspector, no gizmo. *Data:* a tilt could follow a value (a gauge needle, a leaning sign).
- [ ] **Asset creator (bring your own asset):** ambitious. Let users make their own parts: model one in the app (e.g. from boxes, cylinders and other low-poly pieces, like the procedural parts are built) and set up its properties (the inspector fields, colours and accent, default size, whether it can drive along roads, lights), so it shows up in Add to scene like a built-in part. Start from scratch, or from an existing asset (a built-in part or another custom one, copied as a starting point to change). Alternatively, or as well, import a 3D model (glTF/GLB) and give it properties. Needs a design pass and ADRs: where custom parts live (in the world, so shared worlds keep them, or per browser), how the scene schema refers to them next to the registry's part kinds (ADR-0005, ADR-0050), size limits for imported models in local storage (ADR-0006), keeping the low-poly look, and how `/schema.json` describes them. *Data:* a custom part could expose data-driven props too, e.g. a height or colour bound to a value.
- [ ] **Add to scene, next:** favourites, recently used and folding sections are done (ADR-0051). Still to do: presets per part (e.g. "Tall office tower", "Red truck") so one click gives a styled object, and a list/grid toggle once there are many more parts and variants. *Data:* presets could come with a label already bound, e.g. "Warehouse with an orders card".
