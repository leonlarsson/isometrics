## Ideas backlog (2026-10-21)

- [ ] **Turning autosave off:** the save status next to the File menu is done (ADR-0057). Maybe a setting to turn autosave off (per browser or per world), e.g. to try changes and throw them away: it then needs an "Unsaved changes" state in that status, an explicit "Save in this browser" (Ctrl+S's meaning would need a decision), and a warning before closing the tab or opening another world with unsaved changes, so nothing is lost by accident (ADR-0020). Needs an ADR. *Data:* once worlds load live data, the status could also say when the data was last updated.
- [ ] **A big library of parts (owner's idea, 2026-10-26):** there are 91 parts; worlds need hundreds. Each is a part of its own in its category's folder (ADR-0043), variants numbered (ADR-0050). The first catalogues of street furniture, lamp posts, infrastructure and vehicles are done. Still to do: *Nature:* more variants of the trees and bushes (hedges are a fence style, ADR-0103). *Buildings:* a stadium. Then more in every category. The groundwork is done: drawing many of the same part cheaply (ADR-0086), the public parts catalogue at `/parts` (ADR-0087) and Add to scene made for many parts (ADR-0088: category chips, a family's variants on one tile, a list). Then a batch at a time, one category per batch, each part a file of its own, so batches can run side by side. Every part keeps the usual: defaults that draw it as designed, named colours (ADR-0060), lights where they'd have them (ADR-0058), real lights for lamps (ADR-0074). *Data:* the usual per-part bindings (colours, lights, scale) cover most; some invite their own (a fountain's flow, a crane's load, a bus stop's waiting count, a planter's flowers by season).
- [ ] **Fences, next (ADR-0103):** fences are built: a drawn line of their own kind with seven styles (picket, rail, hedge, stone wall, railing, chain-link, guard rail), standing on the ground under them, editing like roads, colours and how much is built from data. Still to do: gates (a gap or a swinging gate at a point, open or shut from a data switch); joining two fences by dragging an end onto another, as roads join; **Fence along the edge** on an area (sides left open); a fence on a bridge following the road's height by itself (a point's lift does it by hand now); more styles (`bollards_01`, `crowd_barrier_01`, a wattle fence, a brick wall); the fence styles in the parts catalogue (`/parts`).
- [ ] **People:** pedestrians as a part, walking along paths, pavements or hidden routes; maybe standing crowds. *Data:* crowd size or walking speed from a value (visitors, queue length).
- [ ] **Helicopters and planes:** aircraft as parts that fly along hidden routes in the air (take-off, circuits, landing on a helipad or runway), using the route heights from ADR-0018. They should bank into turns like real aircraft (roll towards the inside of the curve, more for tighter or faster turns), and pitch when climbing or descending (a helicopter also noses down to fly forward). Banking needs roll, which entities don't have yet (see *Rotation in all three axes*), though it could be applied at render time only. A whole route can already be moved and lifted in one go (ADR-0066). Also an **airport** building (terminal, control tower, runway or apron), plus a helipad, for them to take off from and land at. *Data:* flights per hour or flying speed from a value; passengers per day on the airport.

- [ ] **A picture of a world from an API (owner's idea, 2026-10-31):** an endpoint (say `POST /api/picture`) that takes a world's JSON and returns a picture of it (PNG, maybe WebP), for reports, chat bots, emails, a dashboard's thumbnail or a shared link's `og:image` (ADR-0006). The world goes through `readWorld()` like any untrusted world (ADR-0104), and the request can say how to look at it: a viewpoint or a camera, the size, the time of day, a mood, the theme, the graphics preset, and values or table rows to apply first (or read its links before drawing). Workers have no WebGL, so the likely way is Cloudflare's Browser Rendering (headless Chrome driven from the worker) opening a render-only route that draws the world with the same renderer as Render a picture… (ADR-0084) and hands back the image; check its limits (GPU or SwiftShader, time per request, cost) first. Cache by a hash of the world and options; needs limits on size and requests, and probably a key. Needs an ADR. *Data:* the point of it: a picture of the world as its data stands now, e.g. a daily snapshot posted to a channel.
